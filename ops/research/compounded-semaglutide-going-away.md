## Safety and wording notes
- "Not FDA-approved" on every mention of compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide; FDA's own forms are "Compounded drugs are not FDA-approved" (Q&A, press release) and "Compounded drugs are not approved by FDA" (policy page); quote one.
- "Essentially copies", "regularly and in inordinate amounts" and "pretextual" are FDA's words about Empower's products in WL 738238; they are allegations in a warning letter ("it appears that you produced drug products that violate the FD&C Act"), not a court finding, and not a finding about any provider we list. Do not say Empower supplies any named provider; none of the three provider pages read names Empower.
- The Eden and altRx letters (June 8, 2026) are about marketing claims; do not conflate them with the Empower production-volume letter.
- The B12 point: FDA's 4/1/2026 example says FDA "may consider" a semaglutide + B12 product essentially a copy when route is the same and strengths are "within 10%". Quote it; do not generalise to "all B12 formulations are illegal".
- 503A continues: FDA's own text preserves compounding "for an identified individual patient" where a prescriber "determines and documents" a significant difference, and the "four or fewer prescriptions ... during a calendar month" line. State it as FDA's wording, not as reassurance.
- No timeline promises: "no final determination found as of Nov 18, 2026" is the only status; no "soon", "by Q1", "ends in".
- Dose language: give mg, mg/mL and mL as the letter does; never "units"; do not compute weeks of supply from vial contents (section B stops at per-vial mg). HealthRx's "0.25 MG → 2.4 MG" is a dose range on a provider page, not a recommendation.
- Trial figures: HealthRx's page prints "13.7% mean weight reduction at 72 weeks" and "20.2% mean weight reduction at 72 weeks" and "25.3% of their weight over 88 weeks"; these are the provider's citations, not verified here; if Sloane uses any trial figure it must come from the label or paper with population and time point (STEP 1 68 weeks; SURMOUNT-1 72 weeks), never from a provider page.
- Shortage status wording: "semaglutide injection and tirzepatide do not appear on FDA's drug shortage list (read Nov 18, 2026); liraglutide injection is listed as 'Currently in Shortage'; the 'Semaglutide Tablet' entry is a Rybelsus discontinuation, not a shortage."
- Provider fairness: report HealthRx's $105/$159 vs $125/$199 inconsistency and the index drift factually, as a date-stamped discrepancy, not as a complaint.
- Medical advice: do not tell readers to stop, switch or stockpile; quote FDA's prescriber-documentation language and point to the prescriber.
